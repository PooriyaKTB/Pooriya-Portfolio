Event-Driven Job Scheduler

A cron scheduler where deciding when a job runs and actually running it are two separate parts of the system. Quartz reads the cron expression and fires at the right moment, but instead of doing the work itself, the scheduler publishes an event to Kafka. Independent consumers pick those events up and execute the jobs.

Splitting scheduling from execution means each side can scale and fail on its own. I'm now working on retries and failure handling when a consumer drops a job halfway through.

What architecture taught me about software

Research before you build

A building designed without talking to the people who will use it fails, and so does software. I start with interviews, existing products and constraints, and I don't open an editor until the scope is clear.

Every decision is a trade-off

For seven years I balanced cost, performance, sustainability and deadlines, and brought projects in up to 15% under budget. Choosing between a quick fix and a clean design feels very familiar.

Delivery is a team sport

I led multi-disciplinary teams and mentored junior team members. In software that turns into clear pull requests, useful code reviews and not leaving teammates guessing.
